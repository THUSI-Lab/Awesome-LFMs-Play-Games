01 / THE PLAYER COMES FIRST

Different worlds.
Growing abilities.

A new game changes the rules. A generalist player must carry useful experience with it.

From resource management and hidden information to navigation and real-time control, games make intelligence act. We trace how foundation models acquire skills, turn decisions into actions, and adapt when the world changes.

Our focus is the complete player—and the environments that provide meaningful experience, reliable feedback, and progressively broader challenges.

01 / RELIABLE INTERACTION

Actions have consequences.

Rules, rewards, and persistent state make experience useful. A player should be able to revisit a world and trust what it learned.

02 / EXPANDING CHALLENGE

Experience can get richer.

New goals, mechanics, opponents, and environments expose gaps in capability and create opportunities to improve.

03 / INDEPENDENT EVALUATION

Growth needs a fresh test.

Measure what carries over to held-out games. For learned worlds, check whether their experience improves players in reliable code-engine environments.

04 / EVIDENCE OF GENERALITY

What does
“better” mean?

Depth, coverage, and transfer capture different kinds of progress. A convincing result makes each claim—and its evaluation conditions—explicit.

TWO ROUTES TO IMPROVEMENT

Experience changes
how a player behaves.

01

Learn in the weights

Demonstrations, reinforcement learning, or continued training update model parameters. Track gains, compute, and retention.

02

Adapt during play

With weights fixed, feedback can revise memory, beliefs, and plans. Test the resulting behavior on fresh episodes.

05 / A RESEARCH ROADMAP

From a good move
to a growing player.

Five research targets extend what a player can handle. They describe evidence to work toward; they are not a claim that current agents have completed the journey.

STAGE 01

Master one game

Compose atomic skills into reliable long-horizon behavior across held-out goals, task chains, and seeds.

WHAT WOULD COUNT AS PROGRESS?

Complete long task chains within the time budget and recover from intermediate failures.
